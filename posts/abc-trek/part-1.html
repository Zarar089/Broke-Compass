Pursuit of the Foothills of Annapurna · Part I

Sentinels at the Window

Kathmandu · Thamel

April 2026 3 min read

So… Annapurna Base Camp. Where do I even begin?

The planning started back in November/December of last year, while I was actively prepping for Bali. Eventually, we settled on an April trip—just in time to escape the monsoon and catch the quiet bloom of spring. From then on, it was just a matter of counting days, watching them fall away one by one, until finally, the day arrived.

On a clear Saturday morning, we set out for Kathmandu. About an hour into the flight, the Himalayas began to emerge, first as faint silhouettes, then as something far more imposing. The 8000-meter giants stood above the clouds like ancient sentinels, unmoved and eternal. Among them, the mighty Kangchenjunga revealed itself first, far to the east.

The Himalayas, a vast wall of rock, snow, and ice standing through time, formed from the slow, relentless collision of tectonic plates, it stretches like a natural boundary between Tibet and the Indian subcontinent. A geographical marvel , rising beyond clouds, with their immaculate grandeur.

The first sight of the Himalayas made it evident that we were already in Nepal, and as we continued to take in that surreal view, almost unwilling to look away, we had finally reached Kathmandu at around 11.30am.

The usual airport rituals followed—immigration, customs, SIM cards, currency exchange, and everything in between—and soon enough, we were on our way to the hostel. Our stay in Kathmandu was brief; just a few hours, really, before we would set off for Pokhara in the evening. We booked a hostel room for us at Thamel and headed straight for it.

Thamel, the melting point of Kathmandu, where people from all corners of the world seem to converge into one vibrant whole. Considered the heart of the city’s tourist life, it has everything: shops, restaurants, hotels, cafés, pubs, something for everyone to be honest. It’s the kind of place where wandering without a plan feels like the only right thing to do.

Cashmere shawls, Tibetan beads, terracotta murals, and intricately carved masks spill out from shopfronts. The houses, built in traditional Newari architecture—a rich symphony of brick and wood—create a visual rhythm that feels both chaotic and beautiful. Add to that the narrow streets, the occasional European-style cafés and pubs, and Thamel begins to feel like a world within itself.

Widely known for its nightlife, when dim lights, prayer flags, and a buzzing energy transform the streets into something almost cinematic, the quieter, slower mornings carry a different kind of magic. A lazy stroll through Thamel in daylight, before the crowds and noise fully awaken, is just as mesmerizing, if not more.

We spent the rest of our time gathering the final pieces of our trek—those small but essential supplies that somehow always get left for the last moment. Moving from one shop to another, bargaining a little, double-checking everything, the reality of what lay ahead began to settle in. Among the many stores, Kalapatthar Trekking Store is one I would highly recommend.

With that done, we set off for lunch—simple, satisfying, and just what we needed. By then, the evening plan was already in motion. We finalized our bus tickets at the hostel reception for a 7:30 PM ride to Pokhara.

There was still a little time left, so we returned to the hostel and gave in to a short nap—the kind that comes not from tiredness alone, but from anticipation. And just like that, as the day quietly slipped into evening, it was time to set off for Pokhara…
